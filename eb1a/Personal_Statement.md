### My Original Contributions

Over the past several years, I have identified three fundamental limitations in every commercially available wearable health device and developed patented solutions to each:

**The Waterproofing Problem.** Every wearable device — from the original Apple Watch to the latest Garmin Fenix — requires a dedicated charging port that creates a water ingress point. Manufacturers address this with rubber gaskets, magnetic charging pucks, and proprietary connectors, but the fundamental tradeoff between waterproofing and charging convenience has never been solved. My Zero-Hole Architecture (ZHA) solves this by making the wristband clasp itself a dual USB-C connector assembly. When clasped, the male plug on one end mates with the female receptacle on the other, providing 15W USB-PD charging, USB 2.0 data transfer, and ECG signal routing. The device body is fully sealed with no dedicated ports. This is protected under both U.S. App. No. 64/073,334 and 64/076,078.

**The Single-Function Electrode Problem.** Existing wearable devices that measure muscle signals (sEMG) — such as the Myo armband and Meta's EMG wristband — cannot deliver therapeutic electrical stimulation. Existing TENS therapy devices — such as PowerDot and Compex — cannot sense muscle signals. No device uses the same electrode array for both functions. My Bidirectional Neuromuscular Electrode Array (BNEA) uses six platinum electrodes on the inner strap surface that switch between sEMG sensing (16 gestures, 94.3% accuracy, 15 ms latency) and TENS therapy delivery (5 clinical protocols) under firmware control. This is protected under U.S. App. No. 64/076,078.

**The Breath Analysis Problem.** Blood alcohol content (BAC) and volatile organic compound (VOC) biomarkers in exhaled breath provide valuable information about metabolic health, liver function, and kidney function. Existing wrist-worn BAC devices (such as BACtrack Skyn) measure transdermal alcohol through the skin — a method that is slow (30–60 minute lag), inaccurate (±0.02% error), and affected by skin temperature and perspiration. My Integrated Breath Analysis Channel (IBAC) uses a Venturi microchannel in the USB-C latch housing to concentrate exhaled breath through a PTFE membrane onto a fuel cell BAC sensor (±0.01% accuracy, real-time) and a MOx VOC sensor (acetone, ammonia, hydrogen sulfide). This is the first wrist-worn device to perform real-time breath-based BAC and biomarker analysis without a mouthpiece. This is protected under U.S. App. No. 64/076,078.

### How My Work Benefits the United States

The United States is the world's largest market for wearable health devices, with over 100 million Americans using some form of wearable health technology. My work addresses three specific needs that benefit the United States:

**Public Health.** The HEALTH-BAND Neuro's Integrated Breath Analysis Channel provides real-time BAC monitoring that could reduce drunk driving fatalities. The United States loses approximately 13,500 lives per year to drunk driving (NHTSA, 2023). A wrist-worn device that continuously monitors BAC and alerts the wearer — or their vehicle — before they drive could prevent thousands of deaths annually.

**Healthcare Cost Reduction.** The HEALTH-BAND Neuro's Bidirectional Neuromuscular Electrode Array combines gesture-sensing and TENS therapy in a single device. TENS therapy is used by over 30 million Americans for pain management, rehabilitation, and neurological conditions. Combining TENS with continuous health monitoring in a single wearable device reduces the cost and complexity of at-home rehabilitation, potentially reducing healthcare costs for Medicare, Medicaid, and private insurers.

**Technology Leadership.** The Zero-Hole Architecture is a fundamental advance in wearable device design that, once patented and commercialized, could be licensed to major wearable device manufacturers — including Apple, Samsung, Garmin, and Fitbit — generating royalty revenue in the United States and establishing U.S. intellectual property leadership in the global wearable device market.
