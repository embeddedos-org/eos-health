## 1. Zenodo (CERN) & Academia.edu
**Title:** HEALTH-KEY ULTRA: Repurposing Standardized Hardware Interfaces for High-Fidelity Biometric Acquisition
**Authors:** Srikanth Patchava (Embedded Operating Systems Research Foundation)
**Abstract / Description:**
This paper presents the HEALTH-KEY ULTRA, a novel ultra-compact wearable health monitor that utilizes a dual-purpose USB Type-C interface. By implementing an active switching matrix, the device repurposes the grounded metal shield of the USB-C connector as a high-impedance analog input. This allows for single-lead electrocardiogram (ECG) and Blood Alcohol Content (BAC) acquisition without requiring dedicated, vulnerable sensor windows. The architecture also includes 64GB of local NAND flash storage, enumerating as a USB Mass Storage Class (MSC) device, ensuring data sovereignty and edge-computing capabilities.
**Keywords/Tags:** Wearable Technology, USB-C, Biometric Sensing, Edge AI, Open-Source Hardware, ECG, BAC.

## 4. LinkedIn Article
**Headline:** The End of Cloud-Dependent Wearables: Introducing the HEALTH-KEY ULTRA
**Body Copy:**
For too long, wearable health technology has relied on a flawed premise: that your most sensitive biometric data must be continuously streamed to a third-party cloud server for processing. 

At the Embedded Operating Systems Research Foundation (EoS), we are challenging this paradigm. Today, we are unveiling the research behind the **HEALTH-KEY ULTRA**, a keychain-sized device that puts data sovereignty first.

By integrating 64GB of local NAND flash and running TensorFlow Lite Micro directly on the nRF52840 MCU, the HEALTH-KEY ULTRA performs anomaly detection (like arrhythmia classification) at the edge. But the real breakthrough is physical: we've developed a hardware topology that repurposes the standard USB-C shield as a clinical-grade analog sensor input for ECG and breath analysis, eliminating the need for fragile sensor windows.

## 5. Rock Health / Digital Health Wire (Press Release)
**FOR IMMEDIATE RELEASE**
**Embedded Operating Systems Research Foundation Unveils HEALTH-KEY ULTRA, Repurposing USB-C for Clinical-Grade Biometrics**

**[City, Date]** – The Embedded Operating Systems Research Foundation (EoS), a 501(c)(3) nonprofit, today announced the publication of the architectural white paper for the HEALTH-KEY ULTRA, a revolutionary approach to ultra-compact health monitoring.

Unlike traditional wearables that rely on fragile optical windows and continuous cloud connectivity, the HEALTH-KEY ULTRA introduces a patented hardware topology (US Provisional App. No. 64/073,334) that utilizes the metallic shield of a standard USB Type-C connector as an active analog sensor. This allows the keychain-sized device to acquire single-lead ECG and Blood Alcohol Content (BAC) readings directly through the charging port.

"We are fundamentally rethinking how biometric data is acquired and stored," said Srikanth Patchava, Lead Researcher at EoS. "By combining this novel sensor approach with 64GB of local storage and on-device AI inference, we are giving users complete sovereignty over their health data."

The complete research portfolio, including open-source firmware and hardware schematics, is available via the EoS GitHub repository.
